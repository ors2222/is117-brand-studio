BRAND-001 / paired hero explorations

One shirt. Two visual directions.

The product image, headline, supporting copy, and action are identical in both previews. Only the visual treatment changes.

The working name is provisional. The flat shirt illustration is the same starter asset in both directions, not product-specific photography.

01 / Modernist-leaning

Open full preview ↗

Reference and choices

Victoria and Albert Museum: “What was Modernism?”. I inspected the museum's image of Christian Dell's Dell-Lampe Type K (1929): adjustable joints and a clearly organized, practical form.

  • A strict two-column grid and small numbered labels make the product and decision sequence easy to scan.
  • Heavy sans-serif type, rules, and one signal color organize attention instead of adding ornament.
  • The shirt sits alone in a clear product field, leaving the blank surface as the focal point.

Intended effect: let a creative visitor quickly understand the product and imagine their own design on it.

Risk: the controlled, technical layout could feel too clinical for a bold, passionate Creator brand.

02 / Postmodernist-leaning

Open full preview ↗

Reference and choices

Victoria and Albert Museum: “What is Postmodernism?”. I inspected Martine Bedin's Super lamp prototype for Memphis (1981): a wheeled base and bright bulb forms make a familiar lamp read like a playful vehicle.

  • Offset panels, overlapping color shapes, and rotated labels create a deliberate clash of visual layers.
  • A serif italic phrase interrupts the bold sans-serif headline without changing its words.
  • The standalone shirt remains clear while the surrounding frame treats the blank as an invitation, not a finished graphic.

Intended effect: make self-expression feel playful and open-ended for the same creative visitor.

Risk: layered decoration may compete with the shirt and reduce the clarity of the action.

Both directions are web interpretations, not copies of the cited objects. These are exploratory layouts, not approved brand design.
